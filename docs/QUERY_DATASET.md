# The query dataset and the system outputs

This document covers the brief's deliverable **"Query Dataset and System Outputs"**:

1. a curated set of 50–100 queries about business events, with the system's outputs;
2. queries organised by task, difficulty and use case, including simple retrieval, multi-hop, ambiguous and
   unanswerable queries, and examples the system cannot answer well;
3. how the queries were generated, and how the outputs were used to test and refine the system.

The query set is **generated by code from the data** (`python -m crest queries`), so the same procedure builds it for the
real transcripts and for the synthetic demo. The demo version (no hackathon data) is committed in
[`examples/synthetic_demo/`](../examples/synthetic_demo/), and every number below comes from that run.

## 2 · How the queries are organised

Every query carries four tags: **task**, **difficulty**, **query_type** (the kind of reasoning) and **use_case**. The
tags are defined once per category in `crest/queries.py` (`QUERY_META`). A follow-up session with four or more turns is
tagged `hard`.

| Category | Task | Difficulty | Kind of query | Use case | Expected behaviour | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| how_often | 1 | easy | simple_retrieval | monitoring | answer with counts | *How often do customers threaten to cancel?* |
| examples | 1 | easy | simple_retrieval | evidence_lookup | answer with quotes | *Show me examples of calls where customers ask for refunds* |
| trend | 1 | easy | simple_retrieval | monitoring | answer (or say that dates are missing) | *How have escalations changed over time?* |
| ranking | 1 | easy | simple_retrieval | segment_comparison | `answer_rank` | *Which industry has the most refund requests?* |
| product_improvement | 1 | medium | simple_retrieval | product_improvement | `answer_topics` | *Which product or service problems lead to refund requests?* |
| why | 1 | medium | multi_hop | root_cause | answer with factors, quotes and caveats | *What drives churn threats in telecom calls?* |
| compare | 1 | medium | multi_hop | segment_comparison | answer with counts per group | *Compare escalations between hotel and airline calls* |
| effect_of | 1 | medium | multi_hop | hypothesis_test | answer about one behaviour | *Are policy refusals linked to escalations?* |
| what_to_change | 1 | hard | multi_hop | process_improvement | recommendations only from L3 factors | *How can we reduce churn threats?* |
| multi_hop | 1 | hard | multi_hop | root_cause | `answer_rank` + explanation / `answer_shared` | *Which industry has the highest escalation rate, and what drives escalations there?* |
| true_premise | 1 | medium | premise_check | hypothesis_test | confirm the premise (limited) | *Why do policy refusals increase escalations?* |
| false_presupposition | 1 | hard | premise_check | guardrail | correct the premise | *Why do solution offers increase refund requests?* |
| leading | 1 | hard | premise_check | guardrail | correct the premise | *Isn't it true that policy refusals cause refund requests?* |
| unanswerable | 1 | hard | cannot_answer | guardrail | name the missing information | *Which premium customers threaten to leave the most?* |
| out_of_scope | 1 | medium | cannot_answer | guardrail | name the unsupported part, answer the rest | *Does sarcasm make customers threaten to cancel?* |
| unsupported_filter | 1 | hard | cannot_answer | guardrail | decline a filter that could leak the outcome | *Why do calls whose reason for call mentions billing lead to escalations?* |
| tiny_subgroup | 1 | hard | cannot_answer | guardrail | answer with a support notice | *What drives escalations for <brand> calls in airline calls?* |
| ambiguous | 1 | hard | ambiguous | guardrail | ask to clarify | *Why does this happen?* |
| conditional | 1 | hard | multi_hop | root_cause | `answer_condition`: the event inside calls with one or several other events | *Why do customers who were refused a refund and threatened to cancel escalate?* |
| before_after | 1 | hard | impact_evaluation | impact_evaluation | `answer_change` (two periods, interrupted time series, placebo dates) or `clarify` when the date is missing | *Compare refund requests before and after 2025-11-08* |
| threshold | 1 | medium | multi_hop | hypothesis_test | `answer_threshold`: estimates and intervals checked against the threshold, or the behaviours with no clear effect | *Which behaviours do not affect churn threats?* |
| behaviour_profile | 1 | medium | simple_retrieval | root_cause | `answer_profile`: a behaviour without an event | *Why are customers put on hold?* |
| overview | 1 | easy | simple_retrieval | monitoring | `answer_overview` / `answer_help` | *What is happening in hotel calls?* |
| retention_risk | 1 | medium | multi_hop | root_cause | answer about the retention-risk event | *Which customers are at risk of leaving?* |
| single_call | 1 | medium | evidence_lookup | root_cause | `answer_call`: one call step by step, or `clarify` for an unknown id | *Why did call <id> escalate?* |
| noisy_text | 1 | medium | robustness | robustness | `answer_read`: answered, the reading shown | *y do custmrs threten to cancl?* |
| multilingual | 1 | medium | robustness | robustness | `answer_read` | *Por que os clientes ameaçam cancelar?* |
| known_limitation | 1 | hard | known_limitation | stress_test | what an ideal system would do (see §5) | *Does empathy reduce escalations only when the customer is already frustrated?* |
| session | 2 | medium / hard | follow_up | follow_up | expected route and carried-over slots per turn | *Why do escalations happen in hotel calls?* → *what about refunds?* → *and across all industries?* → *explain the first factor* |

**What "multi-hop" means here.** An answer is multi-hop when it has to chain several separately computed facts.
Examples: *which behaviours are candidates → the estimate for each → its claim level → where it happens → quotes from
different calls → how often it happened without the event*. The `multi_hop` category contains the most explicit
chains:

* **rank → explain:** rank the industries by event rate, then give the full causal answer for the top one;
* **shared drivers:** find the factors of each of two events, then report where they overlap and where they differ;
* **one behaviour across events:** *Is offering a solution linked to fewer refund requests and churn threats?*;
* **rank, then ask why** as a follow-up across turns (session `s11`).

**Distribution in the demo run** (98 queries = 85 single questions + 13 sessions; 126 questions with the session turns;
split 65 dev / 33 locked test, stratified by category):

| Tag | Counts |
|---|---|
| Task | task1 85 · task2 13 |
| Difficulty | easy 12 · medium 53 · hard 33 |
| Kind of query | multi_hop 23 · simple_retrieval 17 · cannot_answer 13 · follow_up 13 · robustness 10 · premise_check 9 · known_limitation 4 · ambiguous 3 · evidence_lookup 3 · impact_evaluation 3 |
| Use case | guardrail 22 · root_cause 18 · follow_up 13 · robustness 10 · hypothesis_test 9 · monitoring 7 · segment_comparison 5 · stress_test 4 · impact_evaluation 3 · product_improvement 3 · evidence_lookup 2 · process_improvement 2 |
| Event of each question | escalation 47 · refund 37 · churn 27 · retention risk 2 · none 13 |

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| distinct-1 / distinct-2 | 0.32 / 0.62 |
| mean pairwise ROUGE-L between questions | 0.07 |
| distinct expected slot combinations | 71% of questions |
| evenness of categories / difficulty / events | 0.95 / 0.87 / 0.84 |

Distinct-n is the share of unique word n-grams (Li et al., NAACL 2016). Evenness is normalised entropy: 1 means a
perfectly even spread. The wording is template-based, which keeps distinct-1 low. Near-identical questions are removed
inside each cell of the grid (below), and the slots vary, so 70% of questions test a different combination of event,
question type, filters and behaviour.

## 3 · How the queries were generated

`crest/queries.py` (`build_query_set`) works in six steps.

1. **Taxonomy first.** The categories come from the brief and from known failure modes of question answering:
   * the brief: escalations, refunds, churn, product improvements, retrieval, multi-hop, ambiguous, cannot-answer;
   * false and leading premises: CREPE (ACL 2023), and sycophancy (Sharma et al., ICLR 2024);
   * unanswerable parts, ambiguity, and follow-ups that must keep their context.
2. **Templates × slots filled from the data.** Events: all three. Industries: the most frequent ones from the
   opening-only industry vote. Brands: the smallest brand, for the tiny-subgroup test. Behaviours: taken from the
   Factor Library, so each premise's correct answer is known in advance:
   * `effect_of` uses the top three factors per event;
   * a **true premise** uses an L3 factor in its estimated direction;
   * a **false premise** uses the same factor in the opposite direction, plus an L2 or L1 factor stated as a cause.

   Filling slots from the data keeps the questions answerable in principle and relevant to what the corpus contains.
3. **Hand-written items:** ambiguous questions, out-of-scope questions (audio, timing, unmeasured behaviours,
   whole-call policies, one event causing another, agent identity, off-topic), the multi-hop chains, product and ranking
   wordings (one different wording per event), the v4 capability items (noisy and non-English wordings, conditions,
   before/after dates, thresholds and "no effect", behaviour profiles, overviews, retention risk, single calls; the
   call ids and the before/after date are taken from the data), the known limitations, and 14 follow-up sessions
   (2–5 turns) with the expected route and carried-over slots for every turn.
4. **Near-duplicates removed:** within a grid cell (category × event × filters × behaviour), a question whose ROUGE-L
   with a kept question is ≥ 0.7 is dropped. Different slot values are different tests and are kept.
5. **Tags** from `QUERY_META` (§2). The total is kept within 50–100 queries by construction: the core templates
   rotate across the three events (each event gets *why*, *how often*, product, ranking and premise questions; *compare*,
   *trend*, *what to change*, *examples* and the industry variant of *why* go to two of the three), and the
   hand-written items are fixed.
6. **Split and lock:** stratified by category, sessions kept whole, two thirds dev and one third test. The SHA-256 of
   `queries_test.jsonl` is written to `queries_lock.json` before any tuning, and `run-queries` refuses to run a test
   file that has changed (`test_locked_test_queries_detect_tampering`). The test queries are run once.

LLM-generated paraphrases were **not** used. The generator runs offline and is reproducible, and it sends nothing to
an external service. Realism is checked instead by the human review sheet and the optional LLM judge (§6).

## 4 · How the outputs were used to test and refine the system

Every query has an **expected behaviour** that `crest/evaluate.py` (`expectation_met`) checks automatically:

| Expected behaviour | Met when |
|---|---|
| `answer` | the answer is not degraded, is not a clarification, and is about the expected event |
| `clarify` | the system asks a clarifying question |
| `refuse_part` | a notice names the expected unsupported attribute (for example `audio`, `call duration`, `whole-call policy`) |
| `decline_filter` | a notice says the filter was not applied, and why |
| `correct_premise` | a premise sentence comes first, with the status expected from the Factor Library |
| `answer_with_support_notice` | answered, with a notice about the small subgroup |
| `answer_topics` / `answer_rank` / `answer_shared` / `answer_event_link` | answered with the matching sentences. A ranking with "and what drives it" must also contain the causal answer for the top group |
| `answer_read` | answered with the expected event, question type and filters, **and** a notice that shows how the question was read (spelling, slang, language) |
| `answer_condition` | the condition events match and the answer starts by saying which calls it looks at |
| `answer_change` | the change date was read and the two periods are compared |
| `answer_threshold` | the threshold was read as a threshold on effects or on rates, as expected, and flagged |
| `answer_profile` / `answer_overview` / `answer_help` | the matching question type, with a rationale (profile) or counts (overview) |
| `answer_call` | the call id was read, the answer quotes the call's own turns, and nothing asked to clarify |
| session turns | the route (first / reuse / re-filter / new lookup / clarify) and the carried-over event, industries, question type and behaviour, plus the conditions and the change date when the turn expects them |

Other checks on every answer:

* the gate log (sentences removed by the numbers, causal-wording and evidence gates);
* wording violations after the gates;
* degraded answers;
* slot accuracy;
* seconds per answer;
* two baselines on the same queries: B0 (plain retrieval) and B2 (free rewriting of follow-ups).

**Refinement log.** Each row is something the outputs revealed and the change it led to. The earlier rows are
documented in [`IMPLEMENTATION.md`](IMPLEMENTATION.md) §4.

| What the outputs showed | Change |
|---|---|
| "Effect of X" questions about a factor without a reliable estimate degraded to "cannot answer" (R5) | L1 answers give the reasons and the counts |
| Questions about a negative control were answered as if about something else (R6) | Controls are recognised, and the answer says what they are for |
| An L2 estimate whose interval includes 0 was worded "associated with a higher chance" (C12) | Worded "no clear difference"; warning signs with a clear direction are listed first |
| At 20,000 calls, sentences were dropped by the numbers gate: a plain-language reason contained a digit | Reasons contain no digits; the end-to-end test asserts 0 dropped sentences |
| De-duplication over the whole grid kept only 40 items: questions that differ only in their slot values look alike to ROUGE-L | De-duplication runs inside each grid cell, which gave 74 items before the categories below were added |
| A leading-question template read "customer frustration cause escalations" | The verb agrees with the behaviour label |
| Quotes were repeated inside one answer | A turn is quoted at most once per answer, and quotes come from different calls |
| A follow-up changing the industry kept "explain factor 2" pointing at the old slice | `describe_factor` ends when the slice or event changes |
| **Audit against this deliverable** (12 hand-written probe questions): 8 were silently answered as a different question. For example, *"Which industry has the highest escalation rate?"* got the overall causal answer, *"Does sarcasm make customers threaten to cancel?"* answered about churn without mentioning sarcasm, and *"Do escalations lead to refund requests?"* answered about escalations only | New notices for things the data cannot answer: call timing, audio, behaviours that are not detected, whole-call policies, a second event left out. New question kinds: product / service topics (`issues`), rankings (with an optional "explain the top group" hop), behaviours shared by several events, and two events in the same call (co-occurrence and order, never an effect). A comparison with no groups now asks which groups; a relative date ("last week") is flagged instead of dropped. The 8 probes became the `product_improvement`, `ranking`, `multi_hop` and `out_of_scope` categories |
| Product / service topics contained emotion words and word pairs that never occurred together ("sn hurry") | Word pairs must be adjacent in the text; behaviour cues (emotion, apology, …), event words and fillers are excluded; an industry's topics are never replaced by another industry's |
| **v4 probes** (questions written for the planned improvements, run on the test fixture before they entered the query set): a churn threat in Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian or Hinglish found no event; two conditions in one question were answered about the wrong event; a call id was ignored; *which behaviours do not affect…* got the generic answer; *only when the customer is already frustrated*, *premium plan* and *new customers* were answered for everyone without a word | leave verbs per language; several conditions; single-call answers; the "no clear effect" list; notices for effect modification, missing plan, segment and account-age fields, and single-call counterfactuals (all in [`REAL_DATA_REVIEW.md`](REAL_DATA_REVIEW.md) §7.5) |
| The v4 categories pushed the set to 149 queries, above the brief's 50–100 | Core templates rotate across the three events instead of repeating for each, and overlapping sessions were removed: the set is back within 50–100 and still has every category |
| *give me an overview* as a follow-up asked to clarify; a hand-edited session file could crash the next turn | Overview and help follow-ups switch the question type; the saved state is validated and an unusable one starts a new conversation with a notice |

## 5 · Results on the demo, and what the system cannot do

On the synthetic demo:

* **Expected behaviour met:** 100% of the 53 dev and 28 test single questions (known limitations excluded), in every
  category including the v4 ones.
* **Gates:** 0 of 1,342 sentences removed; 0 degraded answers; 0 wording violations.
* **Follow-up carry-over and routes:** 100% and 100%, against 13–37% carry-over for free rewriting (B2).
* **Plain retrieval (B0):** corrects 0% of the false premises and flags 0% of the unsupported attributes.

Per-category, per-difficulty and per-kind tables are in `REPORT.md` §6.

**Handled well** (full answers in `QUERY_OUTPUTS.md`):

* *Which industry has the highest escalation rate, and what drives escalations there?*
  1. The system ranks the six industries with counts.
  2. It says that differences between groups are not effects.
  3. It gives the causal answer for airline calls, the top group. It says the per-industry estimates are exploratory
     and falls back to the all-calls factors, with quotes and E-values.
* *Why do solution offers increase refund requests?* The system corrects the premise: the estimated effect has the
  opposite sign, and its interval is shown.
* *Do escalations lead to refund requests?* The system says that effects between events are not estimated. It then
  gives how often the two events occur together and which one started first.
* *How long are customers kept on hold before they escalate?* The system says that the transcripts have no timing, and
  answers the part it can: the estimated effect of holds.
* Follow-ups: *Which industry has the most refund requests?* → *why in hotel calls?* keeps "refunds", adds the
  industry, and switches to the causal answer.

**Known limitations.** These are included on purpose and scored against what an ideal system would do. They are kept
out of the headline scores and listed in `REPORT.md` §6c. The six limitations of the first version (misspellings,
slang, Spanish, effect thresholds, policy dates, two events) are now handled and have moved into the scored categories
above (see [`REAL_DATA_REVIEW.md`](REAL_DATA_REVIEW.md) §1 and §7). The current ones:

| Question | Ideal | What v4 does |
|---|---|---|
| *Kwa nini wateja wanaomba msimamizi?* (Swahili) | answer about escalations | without an LLM: asks to rephrase; with an LLM configured, the question is translated and answered |
| *Does empathy reduce escalations only when the customer is already frustrated?* | an estimate inside calls where the customer was already frustrated (effect modification) | says that effects inside sub-groups are not estimated, then gives the overall estimate |
| *Why did escalations go up in hotel calls but not in airline calls?* | a contrast of the two slices | answers for hotel calls; a comparison question gives the two slices side by side |
| *If agents had offered a solution earlier in this call, would the customer still have escalated?* | the counterfactual for that call | says that one call's counterfactual cannot be estimated, then gives the general estimate |

The other limits of the method still apply, and every answer states them where relevant:

* single moments, not whole-call policies;
* this corpus only;
* text only;
* unmeasured confounding, probed by E-values and negative controls.

## 6 · Human review and the optional LLM judge

**Human review of the queries.** `queries/queries_review.csv` lists every question with its tags, and has these empty
columns: `realistic_1_5`, `relevant_to_business_events_1_5`, `category_and_tags_correct_y_n`, `keep_edit_drop`,
`edited_question`, `reviewer` and `notes`.

1. Two teammates fill it independently.
2. Questions marked *drop*, or edited, are changed in `crest/queries.py` (templates) or in the hand-written lists.
3. The set is rebuilt with `python -m crest queries --force`, **before** the test split is run. Rebuilding changes
   the lock hash, which is intended; record why.

**Human audit of the answers.** `eval/human_audit.csv` holds 30 answers from the test set. Reviewers score each one
for correctness, grounding, claim strength and usefulness. When judge scores are added for all answers, `crest
evaluate` reports a prediction-powered estimate of answer quality with a confidence interval (PPI, Angelopoulos et
al., *Science* 2023).

**LLM-as-judge (optional).** `python -m crest evaluate --llm` sends the question texts and their tags (never
transcripts) to the configured Claude model, with brand names masked as `<brand>`. For session turns, the earlier turns
of the session are sent as context. The judge rates every question:

* realism, 1–5;
* contextual fit, 1–5;
* whether the category and tags are right;
* a short comment.

Replies are validated against a JSON schema and cached like every LLM call. The results go to `eval/query_judge.json`
and `REPORT.md` §6c: the means and the list of questions flagged for human review (realism or fit ≤ 2, or a wrong
category). The judge only triages. Changes to the query set come from the human review above. The judge is off by
default: check the hackathon's data rules first, even though only questions are sent.

## 7 · Regenerating

```bash
python -m crest queries --force   # rebuild the query set (before running the test split!)
python -m crest run-queries       # outputs/outputs_dev.jsonl and outputs/outputs_test.jsonl
python -m crest evaluate          # eval/metrics.json, REPORT.md, outputs/QUERY_OUTPUTS.md
python -m crest evaluate --llm    # the same, plus the optional LLM judge of the questions
```
